Your schema lives in three places. The database... the migrations folder... and the types you hope still match.
Kick D-B puts it in one place. You write your tables in TypeScript, once.
Queries are typed from that schema. Load a user with their latest posts in one query, and your editor knows every field.
Change the schema, and run kick D-B generate. You get the SQL to apply, a draft to undo it, and a snapshot of what the database should look like.
Generated SQL waits for a person. Outside development, the runner refuses any migration nobody has reviewed. Read it, approve it, ship it.
At boot, kick D-B compares the live database with the last snapshot. If someone changed it by hand, you hear about it first. Indexes you add on purpose? Leave them out by name.
Transactions commit when your code finishes, and roll back when it throws. Isolation levels and savepoints are there when you need them.
Postgres, MySQL, or S-Q-Lite, with one API. Moving from Prisma, Drizzle, Type O-R-M, or Sequelize? There's a guide for each.
Kick D-B. One schema. Typed queries. And migrations you can read. Run: kick add D-B.
